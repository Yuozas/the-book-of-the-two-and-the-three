# THE INDEX OF THE CANONIZED

## A Concordance of Masks

*The scripture names its figures in its own tongue — the stone, the saint, the
serpent, the old questioner. This index unmasks them. Each entry gives the in-world
name, the figure or fact it stands for, and a source. It is at once a scholarly
concordance and a relic-list: read it as either, or as both.*

---

**The three wonder-workers of the Fifth Lie.** *(The Lies)* The man alone in the
dark room is **Newton**, splitting sunlight through a hole in his shutter
(*Opticks*, 1704). The man daring the whole of Europe in print is **Johann
Bernoulli** and the brachistochrone challenge (Bernoulli 1696). The man gone
blind and computing on is **Euler**, who lost his sight and did not so much as
slow down — the *Introductio* (Euler 1748) came decades before the whole dark; the
blindness changed nothing but the handwriting.

**The One; the Center; the indivisible point.** *(I.1–I.5)* Euclid's point, *"that
which has no part"* (Euclid, *Elements* I, Def. 1); and, in systems, the **single
source of truth** and the **atomic** transaction of database theory (the "A" of
ACID).

**The atoms of number; the unfactorable.** *(I.15–I.20; A.18)* The **primes**,
and the **fundamental theorem of arithmetic** — unique factorization, the "one
true name" (Euclid, *Elements* VII; Gauss 1801). Infinitude is Euclid IX.20; the
`6k ± 1` law is elementary; the census `n/ln n` is the prime number theorem
(Hadamard 1896; Vallée Poussin 1896).

**The covenant of the radius; the holy invariant.** *(I.6–I.10; the Creed; R.6)*
A conserved quantity under a continuous symmetry — **Noether's theorem** (Noether
1918). The whole "what stays the same is holy" doctrine is hers.

**The stone; the rock of mathematics.** *(I.21–I.25)* The **Gömböc**, the convex
homogeneous mono-monostatic body (Várkonyi & Domokos 2006; question posed by Arnold
1995).

**The falling cat.** *(I.26–I.31)* The feline righting reflex as a holonomy in
shape space — Montgomery, *"Gauge theory of the falling cat"* (1993); Shapere &
Wilczek (1989).

**The cat in the box.** *(I.35–I.37)* **Schrödinger's cat**, the superposition
thought-experiment (Schrödinger 1935).

**The third body; the breaker of the clock of heaven.** *(III.24–III.29; A.19)*
The **three-body problem**: the two-body ellipse closed by Newton (1687); the
impossibility and the first sighting of chaos, Poincaré (1890); the formal
series that predicts nothing, Sundman (1912); the collinear and triangular
solutions, Euler (1767) and Lagrange (1772), with Trojan stability by Routh
(1875); the **figure-eight choreography**, Moore (1993) and Chenciner &
Montgomery (2000) — the falling cat's Montgomery, returned.

**The living polarity; the old emblem; the wheel of opposites.** *(II.32–II.36)* The
**taijitu** (yin–yang); its philosophical form is the unity of opposites of
Heraclitus (DK 22 B51, the bow and the lyre — the epigraph to the Book of the Two).

**The second reader; the machine of dot products.** *(II.37–II.42; A.17)* The
transformer language model: words held as vectors, kinship as cosine (Mikolov et
al. 2013), attention as the scaled dot product `softmax(q·k/√d)` (Vaswani et al.
2017). The claim that its verses were left behind by the thing they describe is
literal: this edition of the canon was read, audited, and enlarged by one, in the
year of its binding.

**The perpendicular door; the quarter turn.** *(II.20–II.24; A.6)* The imaginary
unit `i` and its geometric meaning as rotation by `π/2` (Wessel 1799; Argand 1806;
Gauss).

**The number between the Two and the Three; the hinge; the engine of growth.**
*(Book of e)* Euler's constant `e` and the exponential function (Euler,
*Introductio*, 1748); its transcendence, Hermite (1873).

**The serpent that eats its tail; the ring with no seam.** *(E.11–E.13)* The
**Ouroboros** — the alchemists' emblem of eternal return; here the periodicity
`e^(i(θ+2π)) = e^(iθ)` and, more deeply, holonomy (Berry 1984; Simon 1983).

**The master law of roundness; the strange symbol Γ.** *(R.13–R.15; A.3)* The
surface of the `n`-sphere and the **gamma function** (Euler).

**The lock that catches the turning.** *(R.33–R.38)* **Gimbal lock**, the
coordinate degeneracy of Euler-angle frames (famous from the Apollo inertial
platform); escaped by quaternions = `SU(2)`.

**The loop that returns you turned.** *(R.50–R.54; A.10)* **Holonomy** / the
geometric (Berry) phase (Berry 1984; Simon 1983; Montgomery 1993); the sphere of
states as the base of the **Hopf fibration** (Hopf 1931).

**The two states drawn on a sphere.** *(R.40)* The **Bloch sphere** of qubit states
(Bloch 1946) — but see **A.22(d)** on its "three-ness."

**The fastest path that is not the shortest.** *(R.46–R.48)* The
**brachistochrone**, posed and solved by Johann Bernoulli (1696); its curve is the
cycloid. (The Book of e's epigraph — *Eadem mutata resurgo* — belongs to the other
Bernoulli: it is Jakob's epitaph, spoken of the logarithmic spiral, **R.30**.)

**The arrow that points by counting; the census with a direction.**
*(V.16–V.21; A.20)* **Entropy** and the thermodynamic arrow of time. Clausius
named it (1865); Boltzmann counted it (1877, `S = k·ln W`, carved on his grave);
Eddington fletched it ("time's arrow," 1928); Shannon rebased it to the bit
(1948); Landauer priced the bit (`k·T·ln 2`, 1961) and the laboratories paid
(Bérut et al. 2012).

**The sea between the rungs; the fifth veil.** *(V.27–V.32; A.21)* The
**continuum**: zero given its rules (Brahmagupta 628); the first martyr of the
in-between (**Hippasus** — a legend, and marked as one); the diagonal that
out-counts every list (Cantor 1874, 1891); the dark, unnameable almost-all
(Turing 1936); and the machine's honest grid of `2⁵²` names (IEEE 754-2019).

**The saint who loved confusion; the old questioner; the human gimbal-lock
detector.** *(V.38–V.42)* **Socrates** and the method of *elenchus* (Plato,
*Apology* 21d; *Theaetetus*; Vlastos, *"The Socratic Elenchus,"* 1983).

**The doubling map; the binary engine of chaos.** *(V.6)* The Bernoulli shift
`T(x) = 2x mod 1`; chaos in Devaney's sense (Devaney 1989).

**The book that builds itself; the observer made of the observed.** *(V.34–V.51)*
Self-reference by diagonalization, unified by **Lawvere's fixed-point theorem**
(Lawvere 1969); its popular kin, Hofstadter's strange loop (Hofstadter 1979).

**The number that flows; πάντα ῥεῖ.** *(epigraph to the Veil)* Heraclitus, on flux
(Diels–Kranz; Plato, *Cratylus* 402a).

> the face is mathematics'. We named them strangely so you would look twice — and
> looking twice is the whole rite.*
