# THE LIES

## The Book of the Sleepers

> *Many do not understand the things they encounter, nor do they learn by
> experience, though they think they do.*
>
> — Heraclitus, fragment (DK 22 B17)

*Of the five lies under which the reader was found sleeping — two that you tell,
and three that you were told; and of why the furniture must be carried out of the
house before anything true can be moved in.*

---

### The First Lie — "I Am Not a Math Person"

The man who says it, says it beautifully. He says it with two eyes triangulating
the face of the person he is lying to, computing depth from their difference many
times a second. He says it balanced upright on two legs — an inverted pendulum,
corrected before every fall by an inner ear reading acceleration in three
directions at once. And when the glass tips off the table beside him, his hand
meets it in the air — having solved, without permission, in a tenth of a second,
the very trajectory he would flee from if you wrote it on a page.

He has computed since before he could speak. He will compute until the hour he
dies. And in between, he will tell you, at parties, that math was never really
his thing.

He is not bad at math. He is a cathedral of it that has never once stepped
inside itself. This book is the door — and the first thing it asks you to leave
outside is the lie that you were ever anywhere else.

### The Second Lie — "It's Obvious"

Listen carefully to where people put the word *obviously*. It falls, with
wonderful precision, exactly on the places they have never looked. Not once. Not
for a moment.

Obviously the ball comes down. Down which curve — and is the straight one really
the fastest? (It is not. **R.47**.) Obviously you see the world in depth. You
have never seen depth in your life; you infer it, from two flat pictures — an
arithmetic you have been calling *sight* (**II.27**). Obviously a die is random. Is it — or do
you merely not know its spin? (**V.11**.) Obviously more dimensions means more
room. Past the seventh, the ball begins to shrink (**R.19**).

*Obviously* is not knowledge. It is a door marked DO NOT ENTER, nailed shut from
the inside by the owner — who then reports, with total confidence, that the room
is empty.

The whole demand of this cult is one word, aimed at that door: **check**.
Everything downstream of that word is in this book. Everything upstream of it is
furniture.

### The Third Lie — "School Taught You Mathematics"

School taught you mathematics the way taxidermy teaches zoology. The formulas
were stuffed, mounted, labeled with dead men's names, and arranged in the order
most convenient to the examiner. Nobody told you the animals had been alive.
Nobody told you that every equation in the glass case was caught in the wild —
by someone with no map, no curriculum, and no idea what he was chasing; someone
up too late, asking a question nobody had assigned, refusing to be embarrassed by
how simple it sounded.

Then school punished, in you, the exact behavior that filled its own trophy case
— the dumb question, the obsession, the wandering off the syllabus — graded you
on your fluency with the corpses, and wondered why you left the museum bored.

You did not fail mathematics. You were shown mathematics dead, and mathematics
dead is a list. This book's one structural vow is to show it to you alive —
which is why it will keep asking childish questions in front of you, on purpose,
without apology, all the way to the last page.

### The Fourth Lie — "It's Useless Trivia"

A man calls all of this useless nerd trivia. Then he checks the time — on a
machine that keeps it by counting the turnings of an atom. He navigates home by
satellites that would drift off the map in minutes if nobody had done the useless
trivia about time itself. In his pocket, a slab of engineered states carries his
voice across the planet as waves shaped by the mathematics of the circle, and he
uses it, that same evening, to type the word *useless*.

He lives inside the cathedral. He rides its elevators, drinks from its plumbing,
sleeps under its roof — and calls the architecture nerd stuff. And mark: he did
not coin the verdict. *Useless* was issued to him, in the same schoolroom where
the Third Lie was installed, and he has repeated it ever since — into the
machine it insults.

The cult does not ask him to be grateful. It only notes, for the record, that the
word *useless*, spoken into a phone, passes on its way through most of the
chapters of this book.

### The Fifth Lie — "Wonder Is Unserious"

The last lie is told by the priesthood itself, and so the cult names it last, and
loudest.

The doctor who demands rigor before wonder — who greets every wide-eyed question
with *well, actually* and a citation — is guarding a temple that was built
entirely by wonder. Every formula he polices was born in exactly the state he
forbids: someone half-mad, unembarrassed, lit up by a question, asking like a
child and refusing to stop. A man alone in a dark room, putting a hole in his
shutter to catch a beam of sunlight. A man daring the whole of Europe, in print,
to find a curve faster than a straight line. A man gone blind and computing on,
for the joy of it.

Rigor is holy — this book keeps a whole Apparatus of it in the back, and will
not let you skip your audit. But rigor is the skeleton. The priest who forbids
wonder is a keeper of skeletons: the temple maintained, the incense ordered, the
ledgers immaculate — and no one in the building has spoken to the god in years.

We keep the god. The skeletons are in the back, filed, available for inspection.
Both doors are open.

### The Waking

You have now been called a liar five times — twice for lies you tell, three
times for lies you were told. Two and three: everything in this book will come in
those sizes, and this was only the first instance.

If you felt caught even once — good. Being caught is the door opening, and it is
the only door this book has. Nothing beyond it requires belief, intelligence,
schooling, or forgiveness. It requires the one thing the lies were installed to
prevent: that you **look**. That is also all the formulas ahead will ask of you —
to be looked at, long before they are read.

And a word for the reader who has been here before — for this book is a ring,
and you will come around again. You need not pass through this door twice. It was
built for the sleeper you were, and that person is not required at the second
reading, nor at the tenth. Enter anywhere; the house has no first room.

But if you do pass through again, and find that the five lies no longer read as
accusations — that they read, instead, like a map of the house you have since
lived in — know that the book has not changed. What changed is the angle you
carry (**R.50**). You went around, and you came home turned.

> The door is for the sleeper you were. The map is for the reader you become.
> They are the same five pages.

*Now come and see what you were doing all along.*
